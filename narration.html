Payment processing · script review

What happens when you tap your card?

One five-dollar coffee. An approval that returns in seconds. A payout that comes later.

Listen to the revised draft · 1 min 17 secRevised narration · connected, step-by-step explanation.
01

The Tap

What actually happens when you pay by card? Say you're buying a five-dollar coffee. You tap, the reader says approved, and you walk away. But the shop hasn't necessarily received the money yet. That message is answering a different question: can this payment go through?

02

The Request

To answer that, the reader sends the payment details to the shop's payment processor. The processor passes the request into the card network, which routes it to the bank that issued your card. That's the bank that can check your account.

03

The Check

Is the card active? Is there enough money or available credit? Does the purchase look suspicious? If the bank approves, it usually reserves those five dollars. So you have five dollars less available to spend, even though the shop hasn't received them. That's authorization.

04

The Return

Then the answer travels back through the network and processor to the reader. Approved. That round trip usually takes seconds, so the barista can hand you the coffee. But approving the payment and collecting it are separate steps.

05

The Collection

To collect, the shop submits the approved transaction. That's called capture. The banks exchange the transaction details and work out what they owe each other. Then they transfer the funds during settlement. The provider then pays out to the shop's bank account on the provider's schedule, with processing fees deducted.

06

The Two Moments

So a card payment has two jobs: get a decision quickly, then complete the transfer. The approval lets you leave with your coffee while the system finishes getting the shop paid.
