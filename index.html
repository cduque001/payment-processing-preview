Payment processing · script review

What happens when you tap your card?

One five-dollar coffee. An approval that returns in seconds. A payout that comes later.

Listen to the draft · 1 min 08 secNarration review. The animated scenes come next.
01

The Tap

You tap your card, grab your coffee, and walk away. But that little approved message doesn't mean the coffee shop has received your money. So what actually happens behind the reader?

02

The Request

For a five-dollar coffee, the reader sends the payment details to the shop's payment processor. It routes the request through the card network to the bank that issued your card: can this purchase go through?

03

The Check

Your bank checks whether the card is valid, whether you have enough money or available credit, and whether the payment looks suspicious. If it approves, it usually reserves those five dollars, reducing what you have available to spend. That's authorization.

04

The Return

The approval travels back through the network and processor to the reader, usually within seconds. You see approved, and the barista hands over your coffee. But the shop still needs to collect those five dollars.

05

The Collection

To collect, the shop submits the approved payment. That's called capture. The banks exchange the payment details and work out what they owe each other. Then funds move between them during settlement. The shop gets its payout on its provider's schedule, with processing fees deducted.

06

The Two Moments

So your coffee purchase has two different moments: permission to pay, and the money arriving. You can already be drinking your coffee while the payment system is still getting the shop paid.
